Case Study: Cyberbullying
Introduction

Cyberbullying is a form of online harassment where a person is repeatedly targeted through digital platforms such as social media, messaging apps, online games, or emails. Unlike traditional bullying, cyberbullying can occur anytime and reach a wide audience quickly.

How the Crime Happens (Step-by-Step)
The bully identifies a target, often someone from school, college, or social media.
The bully creates harmful content such as insulting messages, rumors, edited photos, or threatening comments.
These messages or images are posted on social media platforms or sent through private messages.
Sometimes fake accounts are created to anonymously harass the victim.
The content spreads quickly when others share or comment on it, increasing humiliation for the victim.
Who Is Usually Targeted

Cyberbullying commonly targets teenagers, students, and young adults who are active on social media platforms like Instagram, WhatsApp, Snapchat, and Facebook. Individuals who appear shy, different, or less socially connected are often targeted more frequently.

Consequences

Cyberbullying can have serious emotional and psychological effects. Victims may experience stress, anxiety, depression, loss of confidence, and fear of social interaction. In extreme cases, it can affect academic performance or lead to self-harm.

Example Incident

A well-known example occurred in the Amanda Todd case (2012, Canada) where a teenager was blackmailed and bullied online after a manipulated photo was circulated. The cyberbullying led to severe emotional distress and eventually her tragic death.
